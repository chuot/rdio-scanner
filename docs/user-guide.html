1. Start here

This page lets you listen to two-way radio calls over the internet. People carry hand-held radios at work — firefighters, for example, or a delivery fleet. The calls they make can be put online for you to hear in a browser. You can hear calls as they happen, and you can find and play calls that already happened. You need no radio, no antenna, and nothing to install.

The people who run the page for you are called the operator. Your operator gives you a web address. Open that address in your browser, and the page appears. If the page asks for an access code, type in the code your operator gave you. This code is how the operator decides who may listen. Nothing else is asked for, and nothing needs to be installed.

Note: How the name is said

Rdio Scanner /ˈreɪ.di.oʊ ˈskæn.ər/ RAY-dee-oh SCAN-er

The machine behind the page, and the choices your operator makes on it, are written down elsewhere, in a guide for operators. Some operators also offer applications for phones and tablets; they work differently and carry their own instructions. What you hear in your browser is the subject here.

2. Your first listening session

Open the address your operator gave you, and type your access code when the page asks for it. The page opens on the main screen, and this is where you will spend most of your listening.

The main screen has three parts. At the top, the page shows the name your operator chose for the site, and a small light sits beside that name. In the middle, a display looks like the screen of a radio. Along the bottom, a control bar carries the buttons you press.

Before any call arrives, the display holds placeholder words: System, Tag, and Talkgroup. Its own name line reads Rdio Scanner v followed by the build's version. Another line reads TGID: 0, E: 0 S: 0, and UID: 0. The frequency line reads F: followed by zeros and Hz, and the time line reads 00:00. Chapter 6 explains these lines one by one.

Press LIVE FEED in the control bar. The button turns on, and the page now follows new calls as they happen. When someone talks and your operator has opened their line to you, the display fills in and the sound plays. The first time this happens can feel sudden: the audio starts on its own, as soon as a call arrives.

The light beside the name tells you what the page is doing. It stays dark while nothing plays. During a call it lights up in a colour. Each line keeps its own colour, so a lit light in a new colour means a different line is talking. While the sound is paused, the light shows a steady ring instead.

That is a whole listening session: press LIVE FEED, listen, and press it again when you are done. The next chapters show you the rest.

3. Choosing what you hear

A system is one radio network that the page carries. The people with radios belong to a system. A talkgroup is one channel within a system. One talkgroup might carry fire dispatch, another the water crew, another the mutual-aid channel. You choose which talkgroups you hear.

Your operator can attach two kinds of extra words to a talkgroup: a group and a tag. These words label the talkgroup for sorting, and the search in Chapter 5 sorts by them. You do not have to do anything with them to start listening.

Press SELECT TG in the control bar. A panel opens over the screen. The panel has its own heading, Select. To close it, press the same button again, or press Close select panel at the panel's edge.

The panel lists every talkgroup you could hear, grouped by system. Each talkgroup has a button with its name. A small light in the button's corner is green when you are hearing that talkgroup and red when you are not.

Press a talkgroup's button to turn it off or on. A talkgroup that is on answers with a short rising tone; one that is off answers with a falling tone. The corner light flips with it.

You can turn many talkgroups off at once. When a system has more than one talkgroup, its section ends in small OFF and ON buttons. Your operator may also sort talkgroups into groups. Then a row of group buttons sits at the top of the panel, and that row ends in its own OFF and ON buttons.

If the server has no talkgroups at all, the panel answers No talkgroup configured! and has nothing to press. That is your operator's to fix.

Your choices stay while you stay on the page: reload the page and the same talkgroups are still on. If every talkgroup is off, the page stays silent. The display holds its last call, the light stays dark, and nothing new arrives. Turn a talkgroup back on, and the sound returns.

4. Following live calls

When the live feed is on, the page brings each new call to your ear as it happens. You hear one call at a time. If a new call arrives while you are still hearing one, the new call waits in a queue. The page plays the queued calls oldest first.

The top line of the display keeps two numbers for you. Q: with a number is how many calls are waiting in the queue. L: with a number is how many people are listening, when your operator shows that count.

If the page loses its link to the server, the display reads NO LINK and the sound stops. The listener count disappears while the link is gone. When the link returns, the readings return with it.

Press PAUSE to stop the sound in the middle of a call. The screen keeps the call, and the call's clock stops at the minute where the sound stopped. Press PAUSE again, and the sound carries on from that point.

To hear the last call over again, press REPLAY LAST. Press it again within one second, and you hear the call before that one. Each extra press steps one call further back.

Press SKIP NEXT to stop the call you are hearing and move to the next call in the queue.

Two buttons hold your attention in place. HOLD SYS makes you hear only the system of the current call, and quiets everything else. HOLD TG makes you hear only that one talkgroup. Press either button again, and the page puts back the choices you had before. When there is no call to hold on to, the button just beeps at you.

Press AVOID to stop hearing the talkgroup of the current call. Each press sets a longer breather: first until you undo it, then 30 minutes, then 60 minutes, then 120 minutes. One more press brings the talkgroup back.

While a talkgroup is avoided for a time, its corner light blinks in the select panel. When the time runs out, the talkgroup turns itself back on. You can also press its button in the panel to turn it back on early and clear the time.

If you leave the page while the live feed is on, your browser stops you first. It asks: Live Feed is ON, do you really want to leave?

5. Finding recorded calls

The server keeps a record of the calls it has already heard. Each one is a recording you can find and hear again. The search panel is where you look for them.

Press SEARCH CALL in the control bar. The panel opens over the main screen, named Search. It starts its search the moment it opens, and the newest calls appear first. When you have finished, press the arrow button at the top of the panel, named Close search panel.

The calls the search answers with sit in a table, ten to a page. The page count under the table tells you which calls you are looking at.

The columns of the search results table, and what each one shows.
ColumnWhat it shows
DateThe day of the call, as month and day.
TimeThe minute of the day the call started at.
DurHow long the call lasted, in minutes and seconds.
SystemThe system the call came from.
TalkgroupThe talkgroup the call was on.
NameThe talkgroup's plain name.

Six boxes let you narrow a search. Sort order chooses which end of the list comes first: Ascending or Descending. The panel starts with Descending. Date asks for calls from around one moment.

Four more boxes name the call's home: System, Talkgroup, Group, and Tag. Each one starts by naming all of them: All Systems, All Talkgroups, All Groups, All Tags. Pick a system, and the talkgroup box fills with that system's talkgroups. Press Reset, and every box starts over.

While a search is on its way, the boxes wait: the page marks them unavailable until the answer lands.

Each row ends in a button named Play. Press it, and the page fetches the recording; the button turns into a spinner named Loading. When the sound starts, the button turns into Stop, and pressing it ends the sound.

Playing a recording leaves the live feed. Press LIVE FEED to go back to it.

Beside the page count there is a switch for download mode. In that mode, every row's button is named Download, and pressing it saves the recording to your device instead of playing it.

A search that answers nothing leaves the table empty, and the page count reads 0 of 0.

6. What the screen tells you

While a call plays, the display answers three questions: who is talking, on what, and how the sound is doing. The lines below answer them in order, top to bottom.

The top line carries the clock, shown as hours and minutes. Chapter 4 explained the counts that share this line.

The next lines name where the call comes from. The first is a small line that can hold the system type. Then the system row gives the system's name, then the talkgroup's type and tag when it has them, then the talkgroup's name or number. The line under that shows the call's date only when the call is not from today, beside a clock that counts the call's minutes.

The system type is the system's modulation or trunking standard. Ten names are possible: AM, DMR, EDACS, FM, NFM, NXDN, P25, Provoice, SmartNet, Tetra. A system can also have no type.

Two of those types, EDACS and Provoice, number their talkgroups in three parts: an agency, a fleet inside that agency, and a subfleet inside that fleet. The listening screens write such a talkgroup in agency-fleet-subfleet form — 11-061 rather than one number — because the three parts are what a listening screen has to show. The administration still asks for and shows the same talkgroup as one decimal number, so the number you type in the administration is the decimal one and only the way a listening screen prints it changes.

The display says the type once when it can. When the talkgroup's type differs from the system's, the system's type shows on the small line just above, so both are named. When the talkgroup has no type, that line carries the system's type, and the type is said once. When the two agree, the type is said once, beside the system.

The large line in the middle shows the talkgroup's name. When the talkgroup has no name, this line shows the frequency instead.

Under the large line, the display prints small numbers and short marks. The table below says what each one shows.

The numbers and marks the display prints during a call.
On the display What it shows
F: The frequency the call came in on, printed as digit groups with the unit Hz.
TGID: The talkgroup's number.
E: and S: Two counts the server keeps about that frequency.
UID: The id of the unit that is talking, when the call carries one.

The bottom line of the display can raise flags. A flag is a small word that marks something about the call or the talkgroup it rides on.

The flags the display can raise.
Flag What it means
AVOID The server is holding this talkgroup away from your ear.
⧖ with a number and M An avoid with a timer: the minutes it has left before it ends on its own.
PATCH The talkgroup is patched: joined with another, so you hear both. This flag appears only together with AVOID.
DELAYED The server holds these calls back for a few minutes before playing them.

Below the flags, the display keeps a history of recent calls: a table with five rows. Its four headings read Time, System, Talkgroup, and Name. When fewer than five calls have come, the spare rows stay blank.

Above the display sits the branding line: the name the operator chose for the site, such as a department name. A small light sits beside it. The page calls this light Server status.

The light is dark while nothing plays. During a call it lights in the colour set for the talkgroup. While you hold a call paused, it keeps showing a steady ring.

A control named Fullscreen makes the display fill the whole screen. Pressing twice on the display does the same thing. While filled, the control is named Exit fullscreen.

The display sits dimmed when nothing is happening. A new call or a pressed button lights it again, and it dims again a few seconds later.

7. Access codes

Some sites ask for an access code before you can listen. Chapter 1 told you your operator gives you one.

When the site asks for one, the unlock screen appears over the main screen. Until the server accepts a code, the page takes nothing else.

The unlock screen has one box, named Unlock code. Type your code into it, and the page tries it. As you type, the code shows as dots, so nobody reads it over your shoulder.

When the server accepts the code, the unlock screen goes away, and live calls begin.

The page keeps the code on your device. On your next visit it sends the saved code by itself, and the unlock screen never appears.

Three things can stop a code, and each one has its own sentence under the box.

When the code has an end date that has passed, the page says This unlock code has expired. Ask the operator for a new code.

When the code is not one the server takes, the page says Invalid unlock code. Check what you typed, and try again.

When the server has no room for more listeners, the page says Too many connections. Wait a while, and try again.

When one of these sentences appears, the box keeps what you typed, so you can fix it. The box also comes back on its own if your code stops working while you listen.

8. On a phone or tablet

The page makes itself at home on a phone or a tablet. It keeps the same parts and moves them to fit the smaller screen. The lines stack, and the control buttons sit in three rows of three.

On a phone or a tablet, your browser can put the page on your home screen, like an application you install. Open the browser's menu and choose its option that adds the page to your home screen. An icon lands there, and it opens the page in its own window.

On a phone or a tablet, ten seconds after the page opens, a notice rises at the bottom. It says the product works natively on that kind of device, and it shows a badge that opens the app store page for it.

Beside the notice, a number counts down from ten. When it runs out, the notice goes away on its own. If you would like the notice again, reload the page and wait.

From time to time the operator puts up a new version of the page. When the page you have open finds the new one, it asks before it uses it. Its title reads New version available, and its question reads:

A new version of the scanner webapp is available. Would you like to reload the page?

Two buttons answer the question. No keeps what you have open for now, and the page asks again next time. Yes reloads the page, and the new version starts.

9. When something does not work

When the page stops doing what you expect, look for your situation in the list below. Each entry says what to try first. If nothing here fits, Chapter 10 says how to ask for help.

No sound at all

Start with your device's volume: the page has no volume of its own. Then check that LIVE FEED is on, and wait a while for a call. The light beside the name lights up when a call plays.

If the display reads NO LINK, the page has lost the server. Reload the page. If that line comes back, the server is not answering, and only your operator can fix it.

One talkgroup stays silent

Press SELECT TG and look at that talkgroup's button: a rising tone means it is on. Then check that HOLD SYS and HOLD TG are off, because either one quiets everything else.

Also check for an avoid. An avoided talkgroup's button blinks with a number of minutes, and Chapter 4 says how to clear one.

The display stays dark

The display sits dimmed until a call arrives or you press a control, so a dark screen can be normal. Check that LIVE FEED is on, and give it time: a dark screen often means no call is coming.

A search finds nothing

A search that answers nothing leaves the table empty, and the page count reads 0 of 0. Press Reset under the filters, and search again with wider filters.

The server keeps recordings only as far back as your operator has set. A call from before that point is gone, and no search can bring it back.

A sound will not stop

Press PAUSE to stop the sound in the middle of a live call. A recording you played from the search panel answers to its own button: while it plays, its row's button reads Stop.

A code that is refused

When the page will not let you in, the unlock screen prints a sentence under the box. Chapter 7 explains each sentence and what to do about it.

A selection that seems lost

Your choices live on the server, not on your device, and reloading the page keeps them. If talkgroups you had chosen answer with a falling tone, the operator has changed the talkgroup data. Open SELECT TG and turn them on again.

If the panel answers No talkgroup configured!, the server has no talkgroups at all right now. Ask your operator.

10. Getting help

When your operator sets a support address on the server, a small button named Help appears under the control bar. When no address is set, that button never appears.

Press it, and a notice rises at the bottom of the page. It reads For further assistance please contact and then names the address your operator set. The address is a link: pressing it starts a new message in your mail app.

The notice counts down from ten and then goes away on its own. Press the button again if you need it back.

If the Help button is not there, no support address is set. You can still ask on the Rdio Scanner discussions page, where the project keeps its discussions.

What to tell your operator

Your operator runs the server and can see what the server sees. When you ask for help, give the details they will need:

  • what you pressed, and what you expected to happen.
  • what the screen showed, such as NO LINK or an empty table.
  • when it happened, and which system or talkgroup you were on.
  • the version line in the name line at the top of the page.
  • the device you use, and its browser.

If the trouble is sound, say whether your device plays other audio, and whether the light beside the name lit up. Chapter 9 lists the usual fixes worth trying first.

What your operator can change

The server keeps recordings for a set span of time, and the search reaches back that far. That span is called the retention. Your operator sets it, along with the few other things you see on the screen.
